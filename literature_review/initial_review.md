## Main gaps appearing across the literature

1. **Insufficient benchmark data:** Many studies rely on small, self-built, or narrowly targeted datasets, limiting comparison and generalization. *(Sources: papers 1, 2, 6, and 7.)*
2. **Weak real-world and real-time validation:** Simulation or controlled indoor tests are more common than continuous, dynamic, operational infrastructure testing. *(Sources: papers 2, 5, and 8.)*
3. **High computational demand:** Large point clouds require substantial memory, processing time, and specialized hardware, which limits emergency or real-time deployment. *(Sources: papers 1, 4, 5, and 6.)*
4. **Limited robustness across conditions:** Noise, environmental conditions, sensor configuration, registration error, and incomplete data reduce reliability, particularly for small defects such as fine cracks. *(Sources: papers 1, 4, and 6.)*
5. **Limited integration:** Multi-sensor data fusion and linking detected defects to structural components or BIM objects remain underdeveloped. *(Sources: papers 1, 4, and 9.)*
6. **Need for standardized evaluation:** Different datasets, metrics, and validation procedures make it difficult to compare methods fairly. *(Source: paper 1; supported by paper 4.)*

## Short research direction suggested by the matrix

A strong future study could develop a standardized, multi-structure benchmark dataset; combine LiDAR, imagery, and other sensors; use efficient point-cloud processing; connect detected defects to BIM or structural components; and validate the system continuously in real operational environments.
